# Morse-cluster source audit for a reproducible benchmark

Read-only extraction from the original SSW paper and the LS-SSW 2024 paper/SI, checked against their archived full-text/SI copies. This is a source audit, not a reproduction run. The best direct candidate in the 2013 paper is the short-range Morse M80 cluster; the paper supplies a useful result/cost benchmark, but not enough information for bitwise or exact-protocol reproduction.

**Assessment.** M80 is a strong candidate for a bounded, source-aligned Morse benchmark because N, potential shape/parameters, temperature, Gaussian width, generic H, force criterion, repeat count, success thresholds, GM energy, and approximate cost are reported. It is not an exact reproduction recipe: the initial-structure distribution, units of the forward-force target, and force-length symbol `σ` remain unresolved. A new implementation must preregister these as explicit deviations/operational choices and keep a separate “paper-reported target” from the local success test; otherwise avoid calling the result a reproduction of the published rate.

## Official Cambridge Cluster Database reference structures

The [Cambridge Cluster Database Morse table](https://www-wales.ch.cam.ac.uk/~wales/CCD/jon/structures/Morse/tables.html) explicitly states that energies are in pair-well-depth units `ε`, coordinate-file distances are in equilibrium pair-separation units `r₀`, and each points file's first line gives its `ρ₀`. Its rho=14 global-minimum rows give:

The 2013 paper does not report an M29 GM energy, so there is no M29 paper-number cross-check. |
| 80 | `80G`, Cₛ | −340.811371 | [points/80G](https://www-wales.ch.cam.ac.uk/~wales/CCD/jon/structures/Morse/points/80G), first line `6.0000000000` | Matches the 2013 Table 1 M80 energy `−340.81137 ε` to its printed precision. The linked coordinates are still rho6 coordinates, not a rho14 point file. |

Thus the official database supplies downloadable **reference shapes** and independently confirms the M80 target energy, but its available per-label coordinate files do not provide the corresponding rho14 optimized geometries. The coordinates are dimensionless in `r₀` units (multiply coordinates by 2.7 Å to use the paper's stated length scale), but that rescaling does not transform a rho6 minimum into a rho14 minimum. They may be used as initial structures only after explicitly treating them as rho6 source geometries and reoptimizing under the rho14 target potential; that reoptimization is outside this source audit and was not run. The table notes that some later entries supersede older papers, but the M80 target value agrees with the 2013 article.

## Height-law follow-up: recoverable through the cited BP-CBD method

SSW2013 §2 Eq7–8 explicitly refers the remaining climbing details to its reference31, Shang & Liu2012, DOI10.1021/ct300250h (archived BP-CBD-user.txt, pp2217–2218). At the trial point R0=Rc+ds*N, the added Gaussian contributes w*exp(-1/2)/ds along N. The cited rule sets the total projected force to0.1. Algebraically, w=ds*exp(1/2)*(F_target - (F_real+F_prior_bias) dot N). Thus per-step numerical heights are not a missing independent fixed parameter: the adaptive rule is supplied by the citation. The M80-specific physical/reduced-unit realization of F_target is not explicit. The existing paper_reference.py implementation at its weight calculation implements this algebra with config.forward_force; no new height mechanism is needed. Do not import the BP-CBD TS stopping criteria into SSW.
