# Fixed proposal energy-trace evidence

## Execution/protocol facts

The published CUDA ledger contains 32 frozen task/backend traces. Every row closes the observer ledger exactly: trace-record count = force-evaluation count = Relaxer telemetry count; every curve value is finite; and all `accepted_state` labels are present. The exact raw input is `output/summary.json` with SHA-256 `652d834cc0ddfec7994094e13b34f0de1101f1e2cb5227e8867e13574be064a7`. It was generated by source-run commit `28e2a56b80c92dfd32c80cc4e41eefb1c7e53d96` from source-summary hash `62cc771e2aa24e9addef0e870d0524f901f02bddc34152cf2a2eeea91a671b04`.

The historical cap-400 run is descriptive only. It has 0/32 bitwise-strict matches, while certificates and termination reasons agree in 32/32 and 32/32 rows. The cross-process float32 differences are reported rather than tuned away; they are not a failure of the present observer ledger.

## Optimizer evidence for this fixed matrix

Across the paired 16 tasks, FIRE used 3053 exact force evaluations and 60.115 s; safe L-BFGS used 1483 and 27.743 s. In this matrix that is 51.4% fewer evaluator calls and 53.9% less wall time for safe L-BFGS. The force certificate holds for 16/16 safe traces and 14/16 FIRE traces.

FIRE reached the 400-iteration observation cap on 2 traces: c60/c60-seed-44-bias-1, pdo/pdo-seed-46-bias-1. Safe L-BFGS had 27 callback-nonaccepted evaluations and 27 telemetry-reported rejected trials; the counts coincide in this ledger. That numerical equality does not establish that the observer label is a general synonym for a line-search rejection.

For safe L-BFGS, callback states have the expected initial-plus-accepted-step count and are monotone non-increasing in total biased energy for all 16 tasks after explicit finalization evaluations are removed. There are 2 positive finalization rechecks among 8 explicit rechecks: pdo/pdo-seed-47-bias-1 (6.1035156e-05 eV), pdo/pdo-seed-48-bias-1 (6.1035156e-05 eV). These are recorded terminal re-evaluations, not Armijo-accepted optimizer steps. For FIRE, `accepted_state` remains only a callback-observer label; its positive total-energy changes are therefore neither an acceptance-rule failure nor a comparable line-search statistic.

## What this does not establish

This observer-only matrix does not establish a general physical or algorithmic superiority of L-BFGS, a causal role for its memory, Armijo test, or step cap, or same-basin acceleration: endpoints can differ. It also does not change the production optimizer, its parameters, the bias PES, or the search policy. The next clean causal experiment is to freeze these tasks and ablate one safe-L-BFGS mechanism at a time while retaining the same observer and exact budget ledger.
